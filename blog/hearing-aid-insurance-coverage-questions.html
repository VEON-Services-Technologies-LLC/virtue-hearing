Questions to Ask About
Hearing Aid Coverage

Back to blog
Insurance May 11, 2026 · Updated May 25, 2026 8 min read California-specific

Hearing aid coverage in the United States is confusing — partly because the rules really are complicated, and partly because most patients never get a clear walkthrough. This guide explains what Original Medicare actually covers (and doesn't), how Medicare Advantage differs, how Medi-Cal works in California, what veterans qualify for, and how to use FSA/HSA dollars. It's written for Montebello residents but most of it applies anywhere in the U.S.

Key takeaways

  • Original Medicare (Part B) does not cover hearing aids, but covers diagnostic hearing exams when a physician orders them.
  • Many Medicare Advantage (Part C) plans include hearing aid benefits — coverage varies widely.
  • California Medi-Cal covers hearing aids for eligible adults, with specific quantity and lifetime limits.
  • Veterans who qualify for VA healthcare can receive hearing aids and ongoing care at no cost through the VA.
  • FSA, HSA, and HRA accounts can be used for qualifying hearing aid expenses.

1. Does Original Medicare cover hearing aids?

Short answer: no, not the devices themselves. According to Medicare.gov, Original Medicare (Part A and Part B) does not pay for hearing aids or routine hearing exams. However, Part B does cover diagnostic hearing and balance exams when your doctor or other healthcare provider orders them to determine whether you need medical treatment.

This distinction matters: a hearing test that's part of evaluating dizziness or sudden hearing loss is usually covered. A routine annual screening is typically not. Ask whether the visit will be coded as diagnostic or screening before you book.

2. What about Medicare Advantage (Part C)?

This is where many people find coverage. Most Medicare Advantage plans — which are private plans offered as an alternative to Original Medicare — include some hearing benefits. According to Kaiser Family Foundation research, roughly 9 in 10 Medicare Advantage enrollees have access to a plan offering hearing benefits.

That said, coverage varies enormously. Some plans cover a fitted hearing aid every 1–3 years with a flat copay; others give a dollar allowance toward whichever device you choose. Some require you to use a specific provider network. The questions to ask your plan:

  • Do I have a hearing aid benefit?
  • Is there a specific provider network I must use?
  • How often is the benefit available — annually, every 2 years, every 3 years?
  • Is the benefit a copay or a dollar allowance?
  • Are batteries, chargers, repairs, or follow-up visits included?
  • Does the benefit cover one ear or both?

Our team can verify your Medicare Advantage benefits before you book — bring your card and we'll do the lookup with you.

3. How does Medi-Cal work in California?

California's Medicaid program — Medi-Cal — restored adult hearing aid benefits in 2020. According to the California Department of Health Care Services, eligible adults can receive hearing aid coverage subject to specific limits, including a maximum dollar amount per device and per year, with prior authorization typically required.

Benefits include the hearing test, the device itself, batteries (or rechargeable), the fitting, and follow-up. Specific dollar limits and frequency rules change periodically, so we verify current Medi-Cal benefits at every Medi-Cal patient's first visit.

4. Veterans benefits

Veterans enrolled in VA healthcare with documented hearing loss qualify for hearing aids, fittings, batteries, repairs, and follow-up care at no cost. Eligibility is determined by VA service connection and enrollment priority group. The VA also covers most cochlear implant care for qualifying veterans.

If you're a veteran, the fastest first step is contacting your nearest VA medical center's audiology department. We work with many veterans in Montebello and can help coordinate care that complements VA benefits.

5. Private insurance through your employer

Coverage varies enormously by plan. Some private plans include hearing aid benefits as part of a wellness or supplemental package; many do not. Three trends are worth knowing about:

  • State mandates. Some states (not California, currently) require certain private plans to cover hearing aids for children and sometimes adults. The American Speech-Language-Hearing Association tracks current state laws.
  • Carve-out programs. Some private plans use a third-party "hearing aid program" that gives you access to discounted devices through specific networks.
  • Negotiated discounts. Even without a hearing benefit, some plans negotiate provider discounts that lower out-of-pocket costs.

6. Using FSA, HSA, and HRA accounts

Hearing aids, batteries, repairs, and accessories are qualifying medical expenses under IRS Publication 502, which means you can use pre-tax dollars from a Flexible Spending Account (FSA), Health Savings Account (HSA), or Health Reimbursement Arrangement (HRA) to pay for them.

For many patients, this is one of the most effective ways to reduce the real cost of hearing care, especially if your insurance offers no direct hearing benefit. If you have a high-deductible health plan with an HSA, hearing aid spending can also help meet your annual deductible.

7. Financing options when insurance falls short

When coverage is limited or unavailable, there are still ways to make hearing care affordable. At Virtue Hearing we offer 0% financing for up to 24 months through a healthcare credit partner, and in-house financing for patients who don't qualify for traditional credit. Third-party financing options like CareCredit are also widely used for hearing aid purchases.

Charitable resources are another path. The Lions Clubs International, through local affiliates, has supported hearing aid programs for low-income patients in many communities. Eligibility and availability are local — we can help you explore options that apply in Los Angeles County.

What to bring to your appointment

  • Your insurance card (both sides) and a valid photo ID.
  • Any recent hearing test results, if you have them.
  • A list of medications you currently take — some can affect hearing.
  • A family member or trusted friend, if you'd like a second set of ears for the conversation.
  • Questions you want answered before making a decision.

Frequently asked questions

Will Medicare ever cover hearing aids?

Congress has debated expanding Medicare to include hearing aids several times in recent years, but as of 2026 Original Medicare still does not cover them. Medicare Advantage remains the route most people use for benefits within the Medicare system. Medicare.gov is the authoritative source for current coverage rules.

Are over-the-counter (OTC) hearing aids covered by insurance?

Generally no. Most insurance benefits, including Medicare Advantage hearing benefits, are tied to a prescription, audiogram, and a network provider. FSA and HSA dollars can usually be used for OTC hearing aids since they are FDA-regulated medical devices.

How long does prior authorization take for Medi-Cal?

Typical processing is 5–10 business days from submission. We handle the paperwork on your behalf, which usually speeds it up.

Can I use insurance from a previous employer for hearing aids?

Only if you're still covered by that plan (for example, through COBRA or a retiree health plan). Hearing aid benefits typically reset annually or every few years, so timing matters.

Important. Insurance rules change. The specifics in this article reflect 2026 general guidance and may not match your exact plan. Always verify benefits directly with your insurer or with our team before purchasing a device. Nothing here is legal, tax, or insurance advice.
